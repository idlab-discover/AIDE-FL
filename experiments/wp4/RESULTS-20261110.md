# WP4 sensor training and privacy results

These experiments ran on 10 November 2026, after the project's stated eligible period. They establish real sensor-model training, validation, aggregation and saved-model inference in local experiments. They do not establish a new Kubernetes deployment or a cross-site demonstration.

The [W&B project](https://forge.coreweave.com/wandb/strgen/aide-wp4-smoke) contains the measured training and validation curves, final test metrics, model artifacts, partition records, source snapshots and PNG/PDF figures. Local evidence is retained under `final-report/working/wp4-validation/`. Each `sensor-*` directory contains `wandb-receipts.json` with remote run links and verification results.

There are 38 primary runs and three earlier DP diagnostics. All 41 remote runs
finished with their retained metrics and committed artifacts verified. The total
includes repeated baselines for the encryption comparisons; it is not 41 independent
datasets or experimental repetitions.

## Dataset and comparisons

The [Smoke Detection Dataset](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/deepcontractor/smoke-detection-dataset) supplies 62,630 sensor readings. After removing two duplicate sensor vectors and applying temporal separation, the experiment has 31,254 training rows, 12,465 validation rows and 18,789 test rows. Another 120 rows form the gaps between periods. The model uses twelve sensor measurements; timestamps, sample counters and exported row indexes are excluded from its inputs. The archive is retained under `sensor-source-20261110`, with SHA-256 `9c1fb61d38f5efa0293c3f2c36f7c4af2556ee0a177653adaaaeb1ea78aceff8`.

The principal comparison uses two disjoint simulated participants, three seeds, twenty rounds and five local solver iterations per round. Every seed includes both local-only models, centralised training and Flower FedAvg. All methods use the same training-only scaler and a fixed later test period. Repeated seeds measure solver and participant-assignment variability on that period. They are not independent dataset repetitions. The chronological split was selected after checking class coverage and remains an exploratory benchmark.

Mean held-out scores across the three seeds are:

| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Stratified | Centralised | 0.8682 | 0.7984 | 0.7331 | 1.4096 |
| Stratified | FedAvg | 0.8650 | 0.7791 | 0.7165 | 1.2340 |
| Stratified | Local 0 | 0.8675 | 0.7847 | 0.7189 | 1.2359 |
| Stratified | Local 1 | 0.8620 | 0.7714 | 0.7145 | 1.2340 |
| Chronological | Centralised | 0.8682 | 0.7984 | 0.7331 | 1.4096 |

| Chronological | Local 1 | 0.8185 | 0.5000 | 0.0797 | 6.7289 |

An always-positive classifier obtains F1 0.8185 and balanced accuracy 0.5. The chronological FedAvg result is therefore weak despite its F1 score. The stratified comparison does not demonstrate an advantage over pooled central training. Both findings should accompany the figures. Source directories are `sensor-iid-20261110` and `sensor-chronological-20261110`. Each contains `figures/validation-curves.pdf` and `figures/heldout-comparison.pdf` plus PNG versions.

## Privacy experiments

The CKKS experiment used two clients and twenty rounds. Its maximum per-round deviation from the plaintext Flower aggregate was `1.71e-13`; final predictions matched the corresponding FedAvg run. Encrypted client uploads totalled 26,742,942 bytes across those rounds. This measures ciphertext payload only, not network traffic or end-to-end overhead. The evidence is in `sensor-fhe-20261110`.

Native SecAgg+ completed five three-client training rounds, comprising twenty protocol stages and sixty replies. Protocol replies contained no plaintext model ArrayRecords. The maximum aggregate deviation from Flower FedAvg was `2.31e-6`, with identical final predictions. This is a complete-cohort correctness experiment; dropout and malicious-party behavior were not evaluated. The evidence is in `sensor-secagg-20261110`.

The reviewed TensorFlow DP experiment used three clients, five rounds, fixed feature scaling and one model seed. Its baseline balanced accuracy was 0.5333. Noise multipliers 0.5 and 1.0 produced balanced accuracies 0.5304 and 0.5275. Native accounting reported epsilon 30.1266 and 12.3017 respectively, at delta `1e-5`, after five model releases without sampling amplification. These large budgets and weak discrimination are diagnostic results, not a strong privacy–utility outcome. The evidence is in `sensor-dp-reviewed-20261110`; `sensor-dp-20261110` retains an earlier diagnostic with independent noise.

Central DP is integrated into the TensorFlow/FedAvg runtime. It trusts the server, uses uniform client weights, suppresses private metrics and saves only noisy released models. Its accountant covers one run and must be composed across repeated runs. SecAgg+ currently uses a local bridge to Flower's native legacy workflow. CKKS is an experimental leveled homomorphic-encryption extension with a separate trusted decryptor role, not threshold or bootstrapped FHE. The local experiments do not establish process isolation. See [privacy configuration and trust boundaries](../../flower/PRIVACY.md).

## Software and verification

The compatible upgrade uses Flower 1.35.0, TensorFlow 2.20.0, scikit-learn 1.9.1 and XGBoost 3.4.1. Flower 1.43 needs a separate external transport migration. The upgrade passed an isolated TLS training/evaluation round, application bundle construction and dependency consistency checks. Its evidence is under `flower-upgrade-20261110`; [dependency notes](../../flower/DEPENDENCIES.md) explain the version choices and repeatable check.

Every sensor run reloads its saved model and checks predictions. Tests cover disjoint temporal partitions, privacy accounting and budget refusal, malformed or duplicate client updates, noisy-only checkpoints, native SecAgg+ exchanges and encrypted weighted averages. The existing cluster was inspected but not redeployed. InfluxDB was unnecessary for these fixed timestamped-data experiments. [Run instructions](README.md) reproduce the comparisons and publish their evidence.
